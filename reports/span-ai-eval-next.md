# Next AI recall evaluation for the Repeat2 token model

The current span evaluation covers synthetic mixed documents built mainly from MAGE, EditLens, and papers, plus the CoAuthor mixed-author set built from GPT-3 suggestions. The frozen human-only set gives a stronger false-positive audit, but it cannot measure AI recall. The next useful addition is a balanced, source-disjoint whole-document evaluation with newer generator families. Score it with the human-calibrated threshold already frozen for v4; report AI recall by generator, human false-positive rate by publication, and document-any-false-highlight by length. Whole-document labels measure detection across windows, not authentic boundary localization.

## Prepared fresh candidate

`/mnt/f/pangram-at-home/data/span_ai_eval_candidate_v1/test.jsonl` contains **300 long nonfiction articles** from the authors' [Human Detectors release](https://github.com/jenna-russell/human_detectors): 150 publisher-attributed human articles and 150 recorded AI articles, paired by the original article URL into **150 source groups**. The five AI conditions each contribute 30 articles: GPT-4o, Claude, o1-pro, paraphrased GPT-4o, and humanized o1-pro. Lengths run from 342 to 1,352 words, median 746; these exercise multiple 512-token windows. The build script is `scripts/build_span_ai_eval_candidate_v1.py`; its manifest records source and output hashes. It discards existing detector scores and annotator opinions from the authors' JSON so these cannot accidentally become model inputs. Exact-text and normalized 24-word overlap checks against all three diverse parent splits passed. The candidate has not been scored by our models or added to the active Vast run.

The [authors' paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654) describes 300 articles from GPT-4o, Claude, o1, and human publishers. The release has `generation_model`, original URL, author, prompt ID, and gold label for each item; the exact model revision is **not** recorded. The publisher articles date to the 2024 era, and named bylines do not independently prove every author's workflow was AI-free. Original publishers retain article rights; keep this local research evaluation only, do not train on or redistribute article text. Report the two transformed AI conditions separately from direct generations, since paraphrasing and humanization answer a harder question.

The local RAID `train_none.csv` still has substantial *record-level* unused material. A streaming audit, excluding every `(domain, source_id)` used in our previous RAID frozen evaluation and requiring at least 100 words, found **11,841 eligible source groups**, of which 11,745 have both human and AI rows. All eight domains have all 11 RAID generators represented. For a long-form matched slice requiring both classes to reach 300 words, available source groups are: abstracts 2, books 640, news 919, poetry 403, recipes 430, Reddit 19, reviews 368, and wiki 28. This supports a later fresh 100-human/100-AI-per-domain group holdout and a separate long-form slice focused on books, news, poetry, recipes, and reviews. It remains the **same published RAID source and generation pipeline** already investigated, so it is fresh by group, not a new benchmark family. The official RAID release lists GPT-4, ChatGPT, GPT-3, GPT-2, Llama 2, Cohere, MPT, and Mistral variants; it does not substitute for a GPT-4o/Claude/o1-era test.

The next evaluation order is: (1) score the 300 Human Detectors articles at the frozen v4 threshold, reporting each generator condition and source publication; (2) run the token model over the existing RAID and MAGE sets as **revisited** stress tests, separating long and short texts; (3) only then consider constructing a new RAID group holdout or sourcing additional independently documented modern generations. Keep the human-origin caveats visible, especially for 2024 articles and user-generated web text.
